# Hello People, video content brief

A self-contained handoff for anyone (a video editor, a Reels producer, or an
AI model) making video content for Hello People. Pairs with
`social-media-brief.md` (the still-image side) and inherits every brand rule
from it. If a rule in this document conflicts with the social brief, the
social brief wins on brand identity, this document wins on video specifics.

Owner: Hello People (hellopeople.ca).
Handles: `@hellopeople_agency` on Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn;
`@hellopeople` on X. Contact: info@hellopeople.ca.

---

## 0. The non-negotiables (from the social brief, restated)

- **No em (`,`) or en (`-`) dashes.** Anywhere. Not in captions, not in
  on-screen text, not in file names. Use a comma, colon, period, or
  parentheses. Write "to" for ranges.
- **Human on the surface. Engineered underneath.** Warm face, quiet
  precision.
- **95 / 5 color.** Solid Link Blue `#1D50CF` does the work. The gradient is
  reserved for the logo, one hero cover moment, and the closing CTA card.
- **Voice.** Sell the outcome, not the tech. Plain, human, confident. No
  buzzwords.
- **Sign off every video.** End card with the logo and `@hellopeople_agency`.

Full color values, typography, and brand rules live in
`social-media-brief.md`. Read it once before editing.

## 1. Where video sits in the system

Video is the loudest way we introduce Hello People. Most people meet the
brand through a Reel or a Short, not a website page. So a video's job is
narrower than a post's:

1. **Hook in 2 seconds** or the viewer swipes.
2. **Teach or prove one small thing** (never two).
3. **Move them to the lead loop** (usually "comment AUDIT" or
   "hellopeople.ca").

We do not chase virality with dance trends or hot-take rage bait. Steady,
useful, honest videos compound faster and pull the right audience (business
owners, ops leads).

### Video pillars (same as the social pillars)

Every video maps to one:

1. **Teach** (educational, how AI works in a real business day). About 40%.
2. **Prove** (before/after, a real case, real numbers). About 25%.
3. **Bust** (myths and objections). About 20%.
4. **Invite** (offers, lead magnets, "comment AUDIT"). About 15%.

A good week has all four.

## 2. Formats and specs

### The four video formats we ship

| Format | Aspect | Resolution | Duration | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reel / Short** | 9:16 (vertical) | 1080 x 1920 | 15 to 60 s (sweet spot 20 to 40) | Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, FB Reels |
| **Story** | 9:16 (vertical) | 1080 x 1920 | up to 60 s per card | Instagram Story, FB Story |
| **LinkedIn native** | 1:1 (square) or 9:16 | 1080 x 1080 or 1080 x 1920 | 30 to 90 s | LinkedIn feed |
| **YouTube long-form** | 16:9 (landscape) | 1920 x 1080 (or 3840 x 2160) | 3 to 10 min (or longer for tutorials) | YouTube channel |

### Frame rate, codec, audio

- Frame rate: **30 fps** default. Use 60 fps only for slow-motion b-roll,
  otherwise it looks off.
- Codec: **H.264, MP4**, high bitrate (12 to 20 Mbps for 1080p, 40+ Mbps for
  4K).
- Audio: **stereo, 48 kHz, at least 192 kbps AAC**. Peak around minus 3 dBFS,
  dialogue loudness around minus 16 LUFS integrated.
- Color: **Rec. 709**, sRGB. No LOG or wide-gamut deliverables for social.

### Safe zones (do not put text or important faces here)

Instagram Reels / Stories, 1080 x 1920:
- Top **~250 px**: reserved for the platform UI (username, camera icon).
- Bottom **~350 px**: reserved for the caption, action buttons, and the
  "Follow" bar.
- Left / right **~60 px**: general padding.

TikTok / YouTube Shorts, 1080 x 1920:
- Right **~180 px**: like, comment, share buttons.
- Bottom **~350 px**: caption and creator bar.

LinkedIn 1:1: keep 60 px padding on all sides. Text is fine anywhere in the
center square.

YouTube 16:9: title-safe area is the inner 90% of the frame.

### File naming

`YYYY-MM-DD_platform_format_slug.mp4`

Examples:
- `2026-09-28_ig_reel_lead-followups.mp4`
- `2026-09-28_yt_short_ai-receptionist-demo.mp4`
- `2026-09-28_linkedin_1x1_before-after-invoices.mp4`

Carousels or multi-card stories append `-1`, `-2`, in order.

## 3. The three video shapes we use

Pick one shape per video. Do not mix.

### A. Talking head (founder-led)

The default. One person, real speech, natural light. Frames the founder or a
team member sharing something they genuinely believe.

- Framing: **medium close-up**, eyes on the top third line, ~10% headroom.
- Camera: at eye level, not below the chin.
- Lens: 24 to 50 mm equivalent, no fisheye.
- Background: real workspace (not a fake bookshelf, not a plain white wall).
  Slight depth. Some negative space on one side for text overlays.
- Lighting: soft, from window or one key light + fill. No harsh nose shadow.
- Audio: **lav mic or shotgun**, never phone mic in a room. If you can hear
  the AC, the take is bad.
- Delivery: **spoken like a real conversation**, not read off a teleprompter.
  If it feels like an ad, restart.

### B. Screen recording (product demo)

For "watch it actually work" moments: an AI voice agent taking a call, a
booking automation running, an inbox getting sorted.

- Record at **native retina** (or 2x) then downscale for crispness.
- **Zoom into the part that matters.** Do not show the whole screen for
  more than 2 seconds at a time.
- Voiceover recorded separately (see audio spec above), then laid over.
- Add subtle cursor highlights (a soft blue glow around clicks). Avoid the
  big yellow ring look.
- Speed ramp boring wait moments (loading, dialing) to 2x or 4x.

### C. Text-on-screen only (kinetic, no talking head)

For quick "3 tasks to automate this week" style Reels, or founder is not
available.

- Uses the same warped-grid background from `assets/social/backgrounds/`.
- **Poppins 800** for the statement, **blue highlight block** on the key
  word, one idea per card.
- Keep each card on screen for **at least 1.5 s** (reading speed).
- 3 to 5 cards maximum per Reel.
- Background music at low volume, no voiceover needed.

## 4. The hook rules (first 2 seconds decide everything)

The first 2 seconds are the entire video. Treat them like the headline of a
post.

**Good hook patterns:**

- **The number:** "Three tasks you can hand to AI this week."
- **The wrong belief:** "Everyone says use AI. Nobody says what for."
- **The specific pain:** "You are losing two days a week to invoice chasing."
- **The reveal:** "Watch this AI answer the phone." *(cut straight to the
  demo)*
- **The comparison:** "Same task. A fraction of the time."
- **The confession:** "We used to send follow-ups by hand. Then we did this."

**Bad hooks (do not use):**

- "Hey guys, so today we are going to talk about..."
- "So, um, this is kind of a big one..."
- Any question that sounds like a quiz.
- Anything with "let me tell you why..."

**Show, not just say.** The hook line should be **spoken AND on screen** in
the first frame, or the video should visually start on the payoff (the
product doing the thing).

## 5. Structure (the reel scaffold)

Every Reel or Short fits this shape:

| Beat | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0 to 2 s | Stop the scroll. Line spoken and on screen. |
| **Setup** | 2 to 6 s | Name the problem in one sentence. |
| **Middle** | 6 to 25 s | Teach it, show it, prove it. One idea. |
| **Payoff** | 25 to 35 s | The outcome. The specific benefit. |
| **CTA** | 35 to 40 s | One clear next action. |
| **End card** | 40 to 45 s | Logo + handle. Held 1 to 2 s. |

Keep it moving. Cut every 1 to 3 seconds unless a screen recording is doing
the talking. Never let a static frame sit for more than 4 seconds.

For a **YouTube long form**, the same shape stretches:

- 0 to 15 s: hook + promise of what they will learn.
- 15 s to end: chaptered walkthrough.
- Last 30 s: recap the payoff, one CTA, end card.

## 6. On-screen text (brand-consistent overlays)

Text on video follows the same rules as text on a post.

- Font: **Poppins 800** for the statement, **Inter 600** for support.
- Highlight: key word in a solid blue block `#1D50CF` with white text,
  radius **8 px**, padding **~4% of text height**. Same as the `.hl` rule
  on posts.
- One highlight per card. Never two.
- Contrast: white or ink text only. Never small text on the gradient or on
  a busy shot.
- Text drop: if the shot is busy, add a subtle ink-800 gradient fade behind
  the text at 30% opacity, so contrast holds.
- Motion: text fades in over 150 to 200 ms. No bouncing, no wobbling, no
  arcade animation. Calm.
- **Timing:** text stays on screen at least 1.5 s per line, plus 0.5 s per
  extra line.

### Lower-third (name and role)

Use once, first time a speaker appears.

- Position: bottom left, above the caption safe zone (Y around 1400 to
  1500 px on a 9:16 frame).
- Layout: white pill background, Ink 800 text, **Inter 600** name in 44 px,
  **Inter 500** role in 32 px, muted grey.
- Optional: a small blue circle (dot) or a `hp-team` icon to the left.
- Duration: fade in over 200 ms, hold 3 s, fade out.

Example: **Deep Patel**, Founder, Hello People.

### End card (every video)

Last 1 to 2 seconds. Options:

- **Minimal end card:** white background, Hello People logo centered,
  `@hellopeople_agency` under it in Inter 500, `hellopeople.ca` under that
  in a muted grey. One tagline line above (e.g. "Say hello to less
  busywork.").
- **Gradient end card:** the closing CTA card can use the brand gradient
  behind a white version of the logo. This is the one gradient moment
  allowed in the video.

Always static (no motion) for the last 1 to 2 seconds so viewers can read
it and the platform can screenshot it as the video preview.

## 7. Captions (burned-in subtitles)

**Always burn in captions.** Most viewers watch muted.

- Font: **Inter 700** or **Poppins 700**, size ~54 px on a 1080 x 1920
  frame.
- Color: white text with a black soft drop shadow (0, 2, 6 px, 60%
  opacity), or a semi-transparent black pill behind the text.
- One or two lines maximum, ~28 characters per line.
- Positioned in the middle third of the frame (Y around 950 to 1150 px),
  never at the very bottom where the platform bar hides it.
- Sync tightly to the speaker (word by word, or phrase by phrase, not
  paragraph blocks).
- **Highlight one key word per line** in the brand blue block, matching the
  post rule.
- Never use auto-captions raw. Always fix punctuation, remove filler words
  ("um", "like"), and check that no dash was inserted.

Auto tools that work: CapCut, Descript, Premiere's Text panel. Always
review before export.

## 8. Audio

### Voice

- Recorded with a lav or shotgun mic, indoors, quiet room.
- Loudness normalized to **minus 16 LUFS integrated, minus 1 dBTP true
  peak**.
- Light noise reduction only. No aggressive de-noise, no autotune-style
  effects.
- Compression: light, 2:1 ratio, aim to keep dynamic range natural.

### Music

- Optional. When used, it sits **way under** the voice (about minus 20 to
  minus 25 dB below the voice).
- Choose: calm, minimal, instrumental. Think lo-fi piano, quiet electronic
  pads, soft acoustic loops.
- Avoid: aggressive trap, hyperactive EDM drops, "corporate motivational"
  library tracks, viral trending sounds unless the video is specifically
  riding a trend.
- Licensing: use Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Musicbed, Uppbeat, or free
  Creative Commons only. Never pirate.

### Sound design

- Subtle whooshes on text transitions are fine, at low volume.
- No stock "AI beep boop" sounds. Ever.
- A soft "swoosh" when a highlight appears is OK if used consistently.

## 9. B-roll and visuals

Real over stock, every time.

**Good b-roll to shoot:**

- A phone ringing on a busy desk.
- Sticky notes with tasks written on them.
- A calendar filling up.
- Fingers typing (from over the shoulder, not stock hero shot).
- The Hello People logo on a laptop or a mug.
- A local business owner working their real workspace (with permission).

**Never use:**

- Robots shaking hands.
- Blue holographic globes.
- Green code raining down a black screen.
- Any AI-generated stock imagery that looks fake.
- Circuit boards, neural network nodes, brain-in-a-lightbulb.

### Cutaways

Every talking-head video should have 2 to 4 cutaway shots to break up the
face. Rule of thumb: cut to b-roll on the exact moment the speaker names a
concrete thing ("invoice chasing" → cut to a stack of invoices).

## 10. Color grading

We keep it clean. No stylized LUTs.

- **White balance:** natural. Slightly warm is fine (2 to 4 mireds warm),
  not orange.
- **Contrast:** medium. No crushed blacks, no clipped highlights.
- **Saturation:** normal. Skin tones stay accurate.
- **Optional brand tint:** in a highlight or transition, a very subtle
  blue-magenta tint (5 to 10% opacity) can be laid over a b-roll shot to
  echo the gradient. Use once per video maximum.

If the shot needs a filter to look good, reshoot it.

## 11. The Reel script format (what a script should include)

Reel scripts are talking points, not word-for-word teleprompter text. They
sound like the founder, not like a brand account. Every script includes:

1. **Day / date** (if part of a weekly plan).
2. **Topic and pillar** (Teach, Prove, Bust, or Invite).
3. **Belief** (one sentence: what you genuinely believe about this topic).
4. **Hook** (the exact first 2 second line, spoken AND on screen).
5. **Format** (talking head, screen recording, or text-on-screen).
6. **Beats** (3 to 5 bullet talking points in your own words, not full
   sentences).
7. **Visuals** (a note on b-roll or on-screen text for each beat).
8. **CTA** (one clear next action, spoken in the last 5 s).
9. **End card** (minimal or gradient).
10. **Post caption** (what goes in the platform caption box).
11. **Hashtags** (per platform, from the social brief).
12. **Alt text** (short description of the video).
13. **Music note** (optional style suggestion).

### A worked example (one reel)

**Day:** Tue
**Topic:** Lead follow-up speed
**Pillar:** Prove
**Belief:** Most leads go cold because nobody replies in the first 5
minutes, not because the lead was bad.

**Hook (0 to 2 s):**
Spoken: "A cold lead is not a bad lead. It is a slow reply."
On screen: "A cold lead is not a bad lead."

**Format:** Talking head, medium close-up, real desk background.

**Beats:**

- Old way: someone gets a form fill, an hour goes by, then a rushed reply.
- What actually converts: a reply inside 5 minutes, warm and specific.
- Our fix: a small automation reads the form and drafts the reply. A human
  still sends it. So it stays personal but it happens in 90 seconds.
- Result: we saw a client's booked calls per week go from 4 to 11.

**Visuals per beat:**

- Beat 1: b-roll of a phone with an unread email at the top.
- Beat 2: cutaway to a clock speeding up.
- Beat 3: screen recording of the draft reply appearing.
- Beat 4: text overlay "4 → 11 booked calls / week" (blue highlight on the
  number 11).

**CTA (last 5 s):** "If your leads keep going cold, comment AUDIT and we
will tell you where to put the first 90 second automation."

**End card:** Minimal, logo + handle + "Say hello to less busywork."

**Post caption:**

> A cold lead is not a bad lead. It is a slow reply.
>
> Most people we talk to lose leads because the first reply takes an hour.
> We put a small automation between the form and the human. The reply now
> goes out in 90 seconds. Same person, same voice, way more booked calls.
>
> Comment AUDIT and we will show you where to put yours, free.

**Hashtags:** #Automation #LeadGen #SmallBusiness #AIAutomation
#SalesOps #HelloPeople

**Alt text:** Founder Deep in a home office, saying "a cold lead is not a
bad lead" with the phrase highlighted in blue on screen, then a short demo
of a draft reply appearing on a screen.

**Music note:** Soft lo-fi piano, low volume.

## 12. YouTube long-form specifics

- **Thumbnail:** 1280 x 720, big Poppins 800 headline (max 4 words), face
  or product on one side. Blue highlight on the key word. Logo in a corner.
  No red arrows, no shocked faces.
- **Title:** plain, benefit-first, 60 characters max. Example: "How we cut
  a client's follow-up time from an hour to 90 seconds."
- **Chapters:** 4 to 8 chapters, each 30 s to 2 min, named plainly.
- **Description:** first two lines are the hook and the payoff (they show
  above the fold). Then 3 to 5 bullet points, a link to the audit page
  with a UTM tag, and a footer with the handles.
- **Cards and end screens:** one card mid-video linking to the audit page,
  one end screen with the audit link + the next best video.

## 13. Do and do not (fast checklist)

### Always

- 2 second hook, spoken AND on screen.
- One idea per video.
- Burn in captions, styled to brand.
- End on a logo card with the handle.
- Solid blue does the work. Gradient earns the end card, not the whole
  edit.
- Cut every 1 to 3 s to keep energy up.
- Voice normalized to ~ minus 16 LUFS.
- Zero dashes anywhere on screen or in the caption.

### Never

- Read a script word for word off a prompter.
- Use robot / circuit-board / holographic stock.
- Let text sit on screen shorter than 1.5 s.
- Post without captions.
- Stack two CTAs in one video.
- Use a viral trending sound just because it is viral. If it does not fit
  the calm, human brand, skip it.
- Let auto-captions ship without a dash-check.

## 14. Deliverable checklist (per video)

For each video, hand back:

1. **The master file** (1080 x 1920 MP4 for Reels, 1920 x 1080 for
   YouTube, 1080 x 1080 for LinkedIn 1:1), file name per section 2.
2. **A vertical clip** (9:16) plus a **square clip** (1:1) if we plan to
   cross-post.
3. **A thumbnail** (JPEG or PNG, 1280 x 720 for YouTube, 1080 x 1920 first
   frame for Reels).
4. **The final script** (marked with what actually got said, so we can
   pull quotes for posts).
5. **The post caption** with the CTA on its own line.
6. **Hashtags** per platform.
7. **Alt text** (short plain description).
8. **The sheet row** for the content sheet (same columns as posts, plus a
   "Video URL / drive link" column).

### Sheet row columns (extended for video)

`Date | Day | Theme | Angle | Pillar | Platform | Format | Duration |
Hook | Caption | Hashtags | CTA | Visual / edit note | Video file |
Thumbnail file | Status`

Status: `Draft` → `Approved` → `Scheduled` → `Posted`.
